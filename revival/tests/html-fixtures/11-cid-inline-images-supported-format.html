This Update is a brief summary of the 15 Jun 2023 FDA meeting at which there was discussion and a unanimous vote about replacing the formulas in the current bivalent SARS-2 vaccines with a new monovalent formula. 

 

For the tl;dr: 

All three of the leading vaccine makers for the U.S. market are expected to produce an updated monovalent vaccine that would be granted EUA by FDA for rollout in the early fall of 2023. In Pfizer’s case, the new monovalent may be available as early as the end of July. All three makers will continue to use their existing delivery platforms, and all are prepared to use XBB.1.5 spike, which was previously endorsed by WHO in May 2023. 

 

Expect the following vaccine lineup: 

• Moderna will use their mRNA formula incorporating monovalent XBB.1.5 spike but has also tested an XBB.1.16 spike formula with good results, so could bring forward either one. (See Moderna slide 33, link given below). 

• Novavax will use a recombinant protein particle + proprietary matrix adjuvant formula incorporating monovalent XBB.1.5 (Novavax slide 19, link below). 

• Pfizer will use their mRNA formula incorporating either monovalent XBB.1.5 or monovalent XBB.1.16, according to the following availability (Pfizer slide 18, link below): 

  “Dose distribution can begin as follows, subject to regulatory approval 

  – XBB.1.5 monovalent: end July 

  – XBB.1.16 monovalent: August 

  – Any other formulation: October.” 

 

Aside on Omicron variant names, relationships, and prevalence: 

XBB variants are descended from the Omicron BA.2 lineage. BA.2 is also a common progenitor of both BA.4 and BA.5 variants. The XBB variants are so named because they emerged from a recombination event in an infected person who was simultaneously infected with two different viruses in the BA.2 lineage. XBB.1.5 emerged in late 2022 and was dominant throughout the spring of ’23. BA.4 and BA.5 evolved independently from BA.2, but their genomes for spike are identical. So, in making vaccines that only encode for spike, BA.4 and BA.5 could have been used interchangeably in the bivalent mRNA vaccines from fall’22.

Given this data, you may be wondering why WHO and the FDA are backing a new formula based solely on XBB.1.5. The short answer is that there are only small antigenic differences between XBB.1.5, XBB.1.16, and XBB.2.3. The Vaccine makers showed data (mostly from animal testing) that demonstrate strong cross-neutralizing responses across subvariants using XBB.1.5-based vaccines. This is nicely explained in the Moderna prezo (see link below). Figure 2 is Moderna’s slide 15. Viewing XBB.1.5, 1.16 and 2.3 as contenders for the formula, XBB.1.5 has the least antigenic difference between those two. Choosing 1.16 or 2.3 would leave the greatest antigenic difference (between those two) in play.

What happened at the FDA’s 15 Jun 2023 VRBPAC meeting? 

The FDA’s vaccine advisory committee (VRBPAC) met on 15 Jun 2023 to hear prezos from CDC, WHO, Moderna, Novavax, and Pfizer, plus a summary by FDA staff on FDA’s a priori stance on updating the formula. (Since Jan’23, FDA was meeting behind the scenes with WHO and the vaccine makers, socializing the feasibility and efficacy of a monovalent XBB.1.5 vaccine, and encouraging maker trials of that and other XBB formulations). 

 

The CDC presentations offered data on current variant epidemiology; antigenic similarities of various Omicron sub-lineages (XBB.1.5, XBB.1.16, and XBB.2.3); and (waning) effectiveness of the current bivalent vaccines against XBB. The vaccine makers gave readouts on clinical trials of their new vaccine formulas for effectiveness against recent Omicron variants, particularly XBB variants, and in comparison, against their existing bivalent formulas (Wuhan-1 plus BA.4/5). 

 

The question before VRBPAC was how the FDA should guide the vaccine makers to modify their SARS-2 vaccines for best protection during the 2023/24 “season”. Following the prezos, Q&A, and a public discussion period, FDA asked VRBPAC members to vote on the following resolution: 

 

For the 2023-2024 Formula of COVID-19 vaccines in the U.S., does the committee recommend an update of the current vaccine composition to a monovalent XBB-lineage? 

 

All 21 voting members of VRBPAC voted YES on this question, but there was discussion about unresolved questions and the need for more data. For example: Is the new formula necessary/appropriate for all age groups? What about healthy younger adults? Will there be data showing that, esp. for seniors who want to take Pfizer’s or GSK’s new RSV vaccine, will it be safe to take that shot simultaneously with the new SARS-2 XBB.1.5 monovalent shot this fall?
