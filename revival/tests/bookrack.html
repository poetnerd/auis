WHAT TO READ NEXT: REVIEWS OF GREAT BOOKS

The dog days of summer have a tendency to make people restless, easily agitated, and maybe even a little puckish. It's a prime season for scandals both grave and fanciful. Isaac Butler's "exhaustively investigated and deeply persuasive" history The Perfect Moment considers the explosive reaction to a 1980s Robert Mapplethorpe art exhibit and the questions of censorship that continue to reverberate today. And on the lighter side, Alexandra Vasti's Scandal of the Summer pits outspoken ladies against rakish smugglers in a Regency romance that features delightful banter and un petit assassination plot. Cooler heads do prevail, at any rate, and the well-read ones most of all!

--Dave Wheeler, senior editor, Shelf Awareness

REDISCOVER

Shahrnush Parsipur, "the celebrated Iranian writer whose subversive works of feminist fiction saw her repeatedly imprisoned" under the Shah as well as the Islamic Republic, died July 3 at age 80, the Guardian reported. Parsipur "excoriated the country's patriarchal culture" in novels like Women Without Men and Touba and the Meaning of Night. In 2026, a translation of Women Without Men by Faridoun Farrokh was published in the U.K. and was longlisted for the International Booker Prize. (continued)
